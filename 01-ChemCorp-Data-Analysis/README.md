# 🧪 ChemCorp Inc. Case Study — Data Analysis

## 📊 Project Overview

This project analyzes business performance for ChemCorp Inc., a Fortune 500 chemical manufacturer operating across 48 U.S. states, serving six major industries and offering 11 chemical products.

The analysis was developed as a business-focused Tableau case study to evaluate customer retention risk, industry performance, product profitability, and geographic growth opportunities.

## 🎯 Business Problem

Despite averaging approximately 7% annual growth, ChemCorp recently lost five long-term customers representing approximately 10% of total sales revenue.

The business needed to understand:

- Which industries provide the strongest growth opportunities?
- Which products generate the highest and lowest profit margins?
- Where are the strongest geographic sales opportunities?
- How can ChemCorp reduce reliance on a small number of customers?
- How can the company protect its existing customer base while expanding?

## 🎯 Business Objective

Identify high-growth industries, reduce customer concentration risk, and optimize the product portfolio over the next 12 months to protect future market share and improve profitability.

## 📈 Key Findings

### 1. Paper & Packaging is the largest revenue contributor

Paper & Packaging generated approximately **$21.43M in revenue**, the highest among the analyzed industries.

However, its profit margin was **55.93%**, indicating that its strategic importance comes primarily from revenue contribution and customer value rather than having the highest profitability.

### 2. Machinery & Industrial Goods and Biotechnology offer strong profit potential

Machinery & Industrial Goods had the highest average profit margin at **66.13%**, followed by Biotechnology at **64.76%**.

These industries represent attractive opportunities for targeted growth and investment.

### 3. Paper & Packaging presents an opportunity for customer expansion

The analysis identified an opportunity to increase the Paper & Packaging customer base by approximately **10–15% over the next 12 months** while protecting existing high-value customers.

### 4. Chemical E has the strongest product margin

Chemical E achieved an average profit margin of approximately **87.45%**, making it a strong candidate for continued investment and sales focus.

Chemical C and Chemical G also showed strong margins of **63.17%** and **62.25%**, respectively.

### 5. Chemical A requires strategic review

Chemical A generated an average profit margin of only **1.60%**, significantly below the other products analyzed.

This suggests a need to investigate pricing, production costs, customer profitability, or the product's overall strategic value.

### 6. Several states represent strong sales opportunities

Customer revenue was highest in:

| State | Customer Revenue |
|---|---:|
| California | $4.07M |
| New York | $2.82M |
| Pennsylvania | $2.43M |
| Texas | $2.00M |
| Florida | $1.60M |

These markets represent potential priorities for targeted sales expansion.

## 💡 Business Recommendations

1. **Protect high-value Paper & Packaging customers** while pursuing the identified 10–15% customer growth opportunity.

2. **Prioritize Machinery & Industrial Goods and Biotechnology** for targeted growth initiatives because of their strong profit margins.

3. **Increase focus on high-margin products**, particularly Chemical E, while evaluating opportunities for Chemical C and Chemical G.

4. **Review Chemical A's profitability** to determine whether pricing changes, cost reductions, operational improvements, or portfolio restructuring are required.

5. **Prioritize geographic sales expansion** in high-revenue markets including California, New York, Pennsylvania, Texas, and Florida.

6. **Reduce customer concentration risk** by expanding the customer base across industries and geographic markets rather than relying heavily on a small number of long-term customers.
